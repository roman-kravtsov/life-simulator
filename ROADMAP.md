# Birthright: living roadmap

This is the one active plan. It says where the project stands, what comes next and why, and it is updated in the same PR as the work it describes (`CLAUDE.md`, "Keeping the roadmap current"). It does not repeat what other docs own:

- **`docs/features.md`** owns every feature's description, acceptance criteria and status. This file refers to features by ID and never keeps a second copy of a status.
- **`docs/roadmap.md`** keeps the original milestone map (M1 to M11) of the 165 features it lists (`docs/features.md` now has 168: CHAR-007, WORLD-014 and ADMIN-005 are placed only here). Its M1 to M7 are history now; M8 to M11 are regrouped into the phases below. Where the two disagree, this file wins.
- **`docs/design.md`** owns how it works, and its section 7 is the log of design decisions. **`docs/mvp.md`** is the original MVP definition.

Status markers: `[x]` done, `[~]` in progress, `[ ]` planned, `[-]` deferred, `[!]` blocked. Priorities: **P0** blocks the core game, **P1** high, **P2** important but can wait, **P3** nice to have. There are no dates: nothing in the project gives a reliable pace to plan by.

Last audited: 2026-10-26, against `main` at `263f66d` (PR #19, M7). The audit evidence is in "Audit evidence" at the end.

---

## 1. Current status

Birthright is a complete game loop that works end to end. A player begins a life, is born into one of 163 countries by the odds of birth as far as public statistics reach, plays year by year with a three-point focus plan, answers decisions (35 of the 167 events) with an odds lens, and sees the finished life as tree rings with a chance, choice and circumstance breakdown. Lives autosave and can be resumed, finished lives are archived, and any life replays exactly from its seed and logs. It is deployed to GitHub Pages (every merge to `main` ships; the last deploy, run 15, was green).

Of the 108 original MVP features, 107 are `done` and one (A11Y-002, screen readers) is `in progress`. The audit added two release-gate features to the MVP, CHAR-007 (distinct names, built in Phase A) and WORLD-014 (method note, built in Phase A); A11Y-006 (axe check, done) was moved in from post-MVP later. Lint, typecheck, 2127 unit tests and 29 Playwright tests passed at the audit.

The honest summary is that **the MVP is built but not yet proven by play.** Nobody has played it the way a player would, the authored text has not been read by Roman, the screen reader pass has not happened, and the audit found four things that would hurt a first-time player or the "real odds" promise (section 4). The next phase is about those, not about new features.

## 2. Current MVP definition

The MVP is `docs/mvp.md` as built (M1 to M7, 108 features), plus a **release gate** that the original definition did not have, because "all features done" turned out not to be the same as "ready for strangers":

1. The full loop works at 400 px and 1440 px: start, play to death, summary, start again, resume after reload. (Met, tested in `e2e/`.)
2. Engine, calibration, guard and component tests pass. (Met.)
3. Keyboard play works for the whole loop. (Met, `e2e/keyboard.spec.ts`.)
4. Screen-reader play works. (Not met: untested with a real reader.)
5. Every number shown has a source, and the game is honest about what is data and what is authored. (Met by WORLD-014, which closed D-004.)
6. Nothing in a normal life reads as a bug. (D-001, duplicate names, is fixed by CHAR-007; the rest of this condition still needs a first-time player's play.)
7. Roman has read the authored text and accepted or changed it. (Not met.)

The MVP is released when 4 to 7 are met. That is Phase A.

**Boundary.** In: everything in M1 to M7 plus Phase A. Out, until the MVP is released: every other feature in `docs/features.md`. Not changed from `docs/mvp.md`: the full-scope decision (Roman, 2026-10-23), every country with complete data, yearly turns, English only.

## 4. What the audit found

These are the findings that change the plan. Each has an ID in the debt table (section 8).

1. **Duplicate names (D-001, fixed by CHAR-007 on 2026-10-27).** Every relative's name is drawn independently from a list of 40 or more, with no check against the people already in the life. In 300 sampled lives, 164 (55%) had two living relatives with the same name at some point, and the player's own name was shared by someone in their life in over 1,500 year-ends. A screenshot from the first life played in the browser shows a friend, another friend and a spouse all named Meena. Event text refers to people by name, so this reads as a bug right away.
2. **Choices barely register (D-002).** A default-course life credits 2.6% of what shaped it to choice (49% circumstance, 49% chance). Putting all three points in one area every year, over 400 lives, moves things by modest and uneven amounts: all-in Learn raises skills by about 10 points and the final income percentile by about 9; all-in Health adds about 1 year of life; all-in Rest adds about 4 happiness; all-in Belonging and all-in Earn add little that shows (all-in Earn against all-in Rest roughly halves unemployment and doubles savings at 45, but does not move the player's rank). Learn looks like a dominant strategy. The design says "choices shift the odds but never escape them", which is the right principle, but at present a player may not be able to _see_ the shift. That is the game's central promise.
3. **The game never says what is authored (D-004).** `docs/design.md` records dozens of authored numbers (school shares, spending rates, focus effects, onset chances) and says none is shown as a published statistic. But the sources screen says "every value in the game keeps its source and year", and nothing tells the player that the model between the data points is ours. The excluded 74 countries skew to conflict and fragile states, which matters for "the real odds of birth today". `docs/mvp.md` set "every number shown has a source" as a definition of done. Closed by WORLD-014 (2026-10-28): the sources screen now has a "How this works" section, and the start screen no longer says "the real odds".
4. **Career and household stand still (D-003).** After the first job, work does not change. Marriage does not end. The person's money is two numbers. For a game about how a life unfolds, adulthood (about 50 of 73 years) is the thinnest part of the model, and the in-flight property and investing design lands on top of it.
5. **Nothing is backed up (D-005, fixed by SAVE-006 on 2026-10-29).** Lives lived in `localStorage` and IndexedDB only, with no export. A life can now be saved to a file and loaded in another browser, and the file is the save, so it replays identically.

Smaller: 718 KB (215 KB gzipped) single JavaScript chunk including all strings; `src/ui/store.ts` (1,105 lines) mixes the store with about 450 lines of view derivations; the screen reader and non-Chromium gaps already tracked; the dependabot PR #20 (closed unmerged on 2026-10-27, superseded by the dependency update, TD-010).

### Phase B: Agency and balance (P1)

- **Objective.** Make a player's choices visible and make the plan worth thinking about.
- **Why.** It is the game's central promise (D-002) and it gets more expensive to change once more systems read the plan.
- **Included.** [x] ADMIN-003 balance report (`npm run balance-report`, `docs/balance-report.md`, CI artifact). [x] ADMIN-005 agency probe (`npm run probe`, `docs/agency-probe.md`, CI artifact): for a batch of lives, the effect of each focus area and of each decision's options on outcomes and on the chance/choice/circumstance shares. [x] A first balance pass using it: Earn now shows in the rank, Learn is trimmed so one point on it no longer beats every other area, and the summary says so when choice was a real part of a mixed life. [x] Check how the odds lens and the summary describe a plan that is only slightly better (the summary said nothing; fixed. The lens reads the engine's own probabilities and was not changed). [x] Decide whether the default plan should remain a free pass: Roman decided on 2026-10-29 that part of it counts as choice (the default course now credits 13.4% to choice); see "Open decisions". [ ] Second balance pass (D-006, TD-012): decisions' lasting effects, and the cause-of-death mix.
- **Dependencies.** Phase A's names fix is independent; the balance pass needs the calibration gate (life expectancy within 2 years) to stay green (it did: the default plan is the reference, so the pass changes nothing in the stats of a player who does not touch the plan; only the summary's split of shares changed).
- **Technical work.** Done: `src/engine/probe.ts` (plays lives under each plan and each answer to each decision, paired against the default course, and holds the targets in `balanceChecks`), `probeReport.ts`, `balanceReport.ts`, `calibrateDeaths` (deaths by cause) in `calibration.ts`, `scripts/probe.ts`, `scripts/balance-report.ts`, `.github/workflows/balance.yml`, a guard test (`tests/engine/balance.test.ts`, 400 lives, about 15 s). `FOCUS_EFFECT`: Learn's effect on skills 30% to 20% and on leaving school 12% to 8%; Earn gained 80% on the skills learnt at work (`drift.ts`, working adults only). `CONTENT_VERSION` 2026.10.3 (a replay of a life with a custom plan now differs). The summary gained `mixedChoice` (`CHOICE_NOTABLE_SHARE` 0.15) and four sentences.
- **Outcome.** A player can tell, from the summary and the odds lens, what their attention did. In the probe (1,500 lives per plan): one point moved to Learn adds 4.5 to the income rank at 35 (was 6.6), one to Earn 2.6 (was nil) and 0.8 years of income in savings, one to Health 0.9 years of life, one to Rest 3.4 points of happiness. The default course credits 13.4% to choice (Roman's call: accepting the default is a choice too; was 2.7%) and a deliberate plan 19.4%; the summary names choice in 1.25 of four stats for a deliberate life and 0.8 for the default one.
- **Done when.** The probe's numbers are in `docs/` and in CI (met), no single focus area is best in all measured outcomes (met: `no-single-point-dominates`), and a life played with a deliberate plan shows a clearly higher choice share than the default one (met at 19.4% against 13.4%; **the thresholds in `balanceChecks` are authored and wait for Roman's word**, except the 10% to 15% band for the default course, which is his figure).
- **What the probe found that this pass did not fix.** (1) Most decisions barely move a life: of 45 non-default choices in 300 lives, about 18 move the rank by a point, happiness or belonging by 0.3, a year of life by 0.3 or savings by 0.1 years of income, and none credits more than 6 points of choice (D-006). The big ones, leaving or staying in school and higher education (rank from 8 to 12 points), credit about nothing to choice because their consequence flows through the school and skills paths, which the summary tags as circumstance. (2) Simulated deaths from cardiovascular disease run 7 to 17 points above the source in every sampled country, because the condition nudges to the cause of death are not centred (TD-012).

### Phase D: Adult life depth (P1 to P2)

- **Objective.** Make the 50 adult years carry a story: work that changes, households that change, things that are owned.
- **Why.** See finding 4. This is where the "lives diverge" experience is made or lost.
- **Included (in order).** [ ] **TD-001 money engine rework first** (section 8): Roman chose a full nominal money engine (2026-10-26, in the property design thread; the design is not final), so wages, prices, savings and debt grow in nominal terms. The design check of 2026-10-30 (`docs/money-engine.md`, a proposal awaiting Roman) recommends the shape: real income flows with nominal savings and debt, a seeded macro path per life drawn from the history (World Bank WDI inflation and growth, ILO earnings by age), amounts shown real by default, and five build steps, the first with no change in behaviour. This rewrites the money code and its calibration, needs inflation and wage-growth series per country, and must come before anything that holds assets. [ ] CAREER-005 career progression and job changes. [ ] REL-005 separation and divorce (with REL-007, domestic violence, written with extra care). [ ] MONEY-008 home ownership and the property, investing and inflation design now in progress (Roman chose "mirror of inequality", a Property tab and sales offered by the engine; inflation is open). [ ] FAM-006 inheritance (needs assets). [ ] CAREER-007 self-employment. [ ] EDU-006 adult learning, HEALTH-007 pregnancy risk, HEALTH-009 disability. [ ] Same-sex partners, if Roman decides yes (a new feature ID is needed).
- **Dependencies.** Phase B (so new systems join a balanced model), TD-001, a baseline from the Phase B probe to measure the rework against (the nominal engine must leave the calibrated outcomes of `money`, income rank, and savings and debt within tolerance, then change only what inflation should change), a `CONTENT_VERSION` bump and a save schema bump with a real migration for the money change (the first one).
- **Technical work.** `Person.money` grows assets and liabilities; new effect types in `src/engine/effects.ts`; new streams (`assets`, `market`...); calibration for ownership rates where data exists (World Bank Findex and similar must be checked against `docs/analysis.md` 1.7 before use); store views split (TD-003) before a Property tab is added.
- **Outcome.** Work, marriage and money change over a life, and two people from the same country can end up very differently for reasons the summary can name.
- **Done when.** Per feature, its acceptance criteria; calibration gate still green; a v1 save migrates and loads (test).

### Phase E: Zoom-in quarters (P2) [M8]

- **Objective.** Crisis years play at quarter pace (TIME-003, SIM-008, DEC-007, TIME-005, EVENT-009, TIME-004).
- **Why later.** The yearly loop is solid and calibrated, zoom is a large change to every system, and it does not fix any finding above. It is cheap to keep ready: the RNG streams already include the period.
- **Rule until then.** A new yearly system must express its chances as yearly probabilities that can be converted (`1 - (1 - p)^(1/4)`), so that Phase D does not make Phase E harder.
- **Done when.** Design 3.1.1's triggers Z1 to Z12 are tested and the yearly risk totals match the unzoomed year.
